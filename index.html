Why CO₂ matters

CO₂ shapes the mushroom, not just its growth.

During fruiting, too much CO₂ tends to give long, thin stems and small, misshapen caps. Lower CO₂ gives fuller caps. Growers usually manage this by guessing at ventilation and by checking the room several times a day. Fresh air also dries the room, so CO₂ and humidity have to be controlled together.

Typical starting point, oyster fruiting

CO₂ below about 1,000 ppm and humidity around 85 to 95%. Each species and strain is different, so ALLID profiles are editable starting points, not rules.

Safety first

High CO₂ is hazardous for people entering a closed room.

ALLID warns locally when CO₂ reaches a level you set. If it loses a sensor it falls back to a safe ventilation cycle, instead of stopping or running forever. Mains-powered outputs use certified components and must be installed by a qualified person.
